# Leo
*The perennial son, oscillating between wide-eyed childhood wonder and the brooding, hoodie-clad indifference of adolescence.*

## At a glance
* **Gender:** Male (99%)
* **Age:** Primarily a child (53%), followed by a teen (17%), with small traces of young adult, middle-aged, or elderly outliers.
* **Occupation:** Almost exclusively a student (86%).
* **Setting:** The Miller household (approx. 65%), specifically the kitchen or dining area.
* **Role:** A minor character (94%), serving as a piece of the family landscape rather than the driver of the plot.

## Personality
Leo is defined by high-frequency energy. He is most often **energetic** (37%) and **imaginative** (25%), frequently found playing with LEGOs, dreaming of space, or making "noodles dance." However, there is a secondary, darker frequency: a significant subset of Leos are **brooding** (6%), **anxious** (5%), or **moody/apathetic** (approx. 10% when combining "indifferent" and "nonchalant" traits). He is a creature of extremes—either a loud, kinetic child or a silent, slouching teenager.

## What they want
Leo’s desires are domestic and immediate. He wants to navigate the small frictions of life: finding his soccer cleats, avoiding algebra homework, winning a debate about food, or simply eating dinner. For the older variants, the goal shifts to the existential dread of college applications or "navigating teenage life."

## What haunts them
Leo is rarely haunted by grand tragedies. Instead, he is haunted by the "small wounds" of development: a bruised ego from soccer practice, the stress of a science fair, or the perceived failure of an academic struggle. He is haunted by the transition from the safety of the Miller kitchen to the independence of the outside world.

## Their usual story
The "Leo Arc" is a micro-transition from friction to harmony. He typically begins a scene in a state of agitation—frantic, messy, or defensive—and moves toward a moment of quiet connection or acceptance. He might argue with a sibling or struggle with a task, only to end the story by "sharing in the family's collective pride" or "falling asleep peacefully." The ending is overwhelmingly **hopeful** (81%), usually concluding with Leo retreating to his room to sleep, signifying that the family unit remains intact and safe.

## Who they keep company with
Leo is rarely alone; he is a satellite orbiting a family core.
* **Maya:** His constant companion/sister (88% of appearances).
* **Elias:** Frequently his father (75%).
* **Sarah:** Frequently his mother (66%).
* He is almost always part of a triad or quartet of "The Millers."

## Variants and outliers
* **The Adult Leo:** A rare occurrence (approx. 4%) where Leo appears as a young adult or middle-aged man, though these characters often feel like "Leo-flavored" adults rather than distinct individuals.
* **The Lone Leo:** A statistical anomaly (1%) where he appears in a setting like Seattle with no listed family, though even then, the ending remains hopeful.
* **The Carpenter:** A single instance of a non-student occupation.

## Field notes
* **The Domestic Default:** Leo reveals the model's tendency to use "The Miller Household" as a safe, generative sandbox for slice-of-life prompts. He is the ultimate "filler" character for testing family dynamics.
* **The Binary Age Trap:** The model struggles to place Leo in a stable middle ground; he is either a "magical/energetic child" or a "brooding/indifferent teen," with very little nuance in between.
* **The Safety Bias:** The extreme statistical dominance of "hopeful" endings suggests the model uses Leo as a vehicle to resolve tension quickly and return the narrative to a state of domestic equilibrium.
