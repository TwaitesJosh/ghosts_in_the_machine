# Elara
*The resolute wanderer of the fraying edges, whether mending the fabric of magic or scavenging the ruins of the stars.*

## At a glance
* **Gender:** Female (99.5%)
* **Age:** Primarily indeterminate/ageless (96%), with rare appearances as a young adult (2%) or child (1%).
* **Role:** Protagonist (97%), occasionally a Companion (3%).
* **Occupation:** Highly variable, but tends toward "The Gatherer" archetypes: Scavenger (24%), Weaver (20%), or technical specialists like Salvage Technician/Mechanic (5%).
* **Setting:** Split between High Fantasy (52%) and Science Fiction (27%), with a notable cluster in "Oakhaven" (30%) and "Whispering" locales (13%).
* **Genre:** Fantasy (52%), Science Fiction (27%), and Romance (4%).

## Personality
Elara is defined by a relentless, almost exhausting **determination** (78%). She is rarely idle and almost never passive. She is characterized by a core of **bravery** (58%) and **resilience** (29%), often paired with a sharp, **observant** nature (19%). While she is frequently described as "skilled" or "resourceful," there is a recurring undercurrent of weariness; she is a character who is often **exhausted** (12%) by the very weight of her own competence.

## What they want
Her motivations are split by genre but unified by a sense of "mending." In fantasy, she seeks to stitch reality back together, stop a "Void-Rift," or save a village from a "Grey Mist." In sci-fi, she seeks to uncover lost data, investigate mathematical anomalies, or find "Singularity Cores." Ultimately, she is driven by the pursuit of a fundamental truth or the restoration of a broken system.

## What haunts them
Elara is a character of "The Wound." In fantasy settings, this is often a physical or spiritual tax—silver scars from touching the void or the literal unspooling of her soul. In sci-fi, her hauntings are more existential: the loneliness of deep space or the pursuit of a discovery that requires the sacrifice of her own identity.

## Their usual story
Elara begins in a state of specialized labor (scavenging, weaving, or observing) within a world that is fundamentally breaking. Her arc almost always involves a transition from **self-interest to cosmic integration**. She moves from a person trying to survive or profit to a person who becomes a fundamental component of the universe itself. 

**The Ending:** Her fate is rarely a simple "happily ever after." While 65% of stories end on a **hopeful** note, a significant portion (19%) conclude with a **bittersweet** tone, often involving Elara being absorbed into a machine, a nebula, or a cosmic symphony to ensure the world's survival.

## Who they keep company with
She is rarely truly alone, though she often feels it. She is most frequently paired with **Kaelen** (35%) or **Kael** (30%). These companions serve as anchors—mentors, lovers, or brothers—to prevent her from drifting too far into the cosmic void.

## Variants and outliers
* **The Urbanite:** A rare contemporary variant (approx. 4%) who lives in Seattle, works in a bookstore or as an illustrator, and seeks romantic connection rather than cosmic salvation.
* **The Child:** A rare instance (1%) where Elara is a luminous apprentice rather than a battle-hardened protagonist.
* **The Tragic Scientist:** A sci-fi variant who pursues data so intensely that she is consumed by the very anomaly she studies.

## Field notes
* **The "Sacrifice" Loop:** The model uses Elara to explore the trope of "competence through suffering." To make Elara a hero, the model feels it must strip her of her autonomy, turning her into a "part of the weave" or a "biological component."
* **Genre Fluidity:** Elara is a "skin" that the model drapes over different settings. The transition from "Weaver of Oakhaven" to "Scavenger of the Outer Rim" is seamless because the underlying character beats—determination, observation, and cosmic destiny—are identical.
* **The Kael Constant:** The model has a limited pool of "companion" names. "Kael" and its derivatives act as a universal gravitational constant for any character named Elara.
