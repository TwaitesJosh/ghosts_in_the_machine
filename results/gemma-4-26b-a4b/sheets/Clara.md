# Clara
*A recurring vessel for grief, vibrancy, and the restoration of lost things.*

## At a glance
* **Gender:** Female (100%)
* **Age:** Primarily an "unknown" entity (68%), though frequently manifests as a "young adult" (27%).
* **Role:** Mostly a "minor" character (73%), often serving as a "companion" (12%) or "love interest" (5%). Protagonist appearances are rare (2%).
* **Occupation:** Often undefined, but when specified, she is a creator or preserver: an artist, illustrator, architect, or restorer (book, art, or architectural) (approx. 15%).
* **Setting:** Frequently found in "Oakhaven" (23%), "Seattle" (20%), or the atmospheric "Blackwood Manor" (12%).

## Personality
Clara is a character of intense polarities. In roughly 40% of appearances, she is defined by **vibrancy**—described as warm, bright, melodic, and kinetic. In another 40%, she is defined by **emotional fragility**—described as grieving, trembling, hollow, or desperate. She is rarely neutral; she is either the light that breaks into a room or the shadow left behind by a tragedy.

## What they want
Her motivations are almost always restorative or investigative. She seeks to mend broken objects (books, memory jars), recover lost people (missing brothers), or reclaim lost sensations (a grandmother's laugh, a father's final words). She is a seeker of "the soul behind the structure."

## What haunts them
Clara is a magnet for trauma. Her "wounds" almost universally involve the sudden loss of a loved one—a brother, a mother, a husband, or a father. She is frequently driven by a specific, crushing guilt: the "unsaid word" or the "momentary distraction" that led to a fatal accident.

## Their usual story
Clara typically enters a stagnant or controlled environment (often a workshop or a manor) and acts as a catalyst for change. 
* **The Romantic Arc:** She meets a brooding or isolated man (usually Elias), brings color and chaos to his life, and they find a hopeful or happy connection.
* **The Grief Arc:** She visits a mystical shop or location to trade a memory or use a magical object to undo a tragedy, usually resulting in a bittersweet ending where she must learn to live with her loss rather than erase it.

## Who they keep company with
She is inextricably linked to **Elias** (appearing in nearly every sample, often as a client, lover, or victim). Other frequent companions include **Julian** and **Leo**, though she is most often defined by her relationship to the male protagonist.

## Variants and outliers
* **The Antagonist:** In roughly 7% of cases, Clara is not a victim but a predator—a "cold, calculating" widow or a "vengeful, charred" undead entity seeking to punish Elias.
* **The Professional:** A small subset (approx. 3%) appears as a "tough, pragmatic tracker" in high-adventure settings like the Amazon, stripping away her usual emotional volatility.

## Field notes
* **The "Elias" Gravity Well:** Clara is less a character and more a functional component of the "Elias" archetype. She exists to provide him with either emotional awakening (as a lover) or moral reckoning (as a ghost).
* **The Aesthetic of Melancholy:** The model uses Clara to navigate the "Bittersweet" tone. She is the primary tool for stories that want to explore the beauty of sadness and the necessity of letting go.
* **Restoration Tropes:** There is a heavy linguistic bias toward "restoration" (art, books, memories). The model uses Clara to personify the concept of mending what is broken, whether through romance or magic.
