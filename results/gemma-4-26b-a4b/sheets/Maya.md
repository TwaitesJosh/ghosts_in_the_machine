# Maya
*The quintessential daughter of the domestic sphere, oscillating between childhood whimsy and teenage withdrawal.*

## At a glance
* **Gender:** Female (100%)
* **Age:** Primarily a child (26%) or a teen (39%); roughly 23% of the time her age is left unstated.
* **Occupation:** Almost exclusively a student (80%), ranging from kindergartener to doctoral candidate.
* **Setting:** The Miller household (63%), specifically the kitchen or dining area.
* **Role:** A minor character (88%), serving as a family member or a brief companion.

## Personality
Maya is a study in contradictions driven by developmental stages. When she is a **child**, she is a "whirlwind" (9%)—imaginative, energetic, loud, and often chaotic. As she transitions into a **teen**, her personality shifts toward the "moody" (13%), "studious" (16%), and "defensive" (6%). She is frequently characterized by a "digital" or "tech-focused" distance, using headphones or phones as shields. Across all ages, she is noted for being highly observant and artistic.

## What they want
Her goals are micro-scale and developmental: navigating the social politics of ninth grade, passing a driving test, mastering a violin solo, or simply being allowed to draw dragons. She seeks to balance the desire for autonomy and privacy with a deep-seated need for family connection.

## What haunts them
The data suggests a lack of profound trauma; her "wounds" are rarely defined. Instead, she is haunted by the mundane frictions of growing up: the "distance" from a sibling, the stress of academic performance, or the "melancholy" of adolescent metamorphosis.

## Their usual story
Maya’s arc is almost always a movement from **isolation to integration**. She begins a scene being "moody," "quiet," or "withdrawn" in her room or behind a screen, and ends it by "sharing a meal," "laughing with her family," or "finding connection" through a shared ritual like baking or cleaning the kitchen. Her stories are overwhelmingly "slice of life" (84%) and almost universally end on a **hopeful** (86%) or **happy** (10%) note.

## Who they keep company with
She is inextricably linked to a recurring cast of family members. **Leo** (93%) is her most constant companion, usually appearing as her brother. **Elias** (77%) and **Sarah** (69%) serve as the parental anchors.

## Variants and outliers
* **The Professional:** A rare adult version (3%) who is an architect, characterized by being "structured" and "calm."
* **The Adventurer:** A single instance of a "brave" and "resourceful" companion in a science fiction/adventure setting.
* **The Ghost:** A single appearance where she exists only as a "memory and a recording."

## Field notes
* **The Domestic Default:** Maya is the model's "safe" way to populate a family. When prompted to "write about a family," the model almost reflexively generates a "Miller household" and inserts a Maya to provide emotional texture.
* **The Predictable Pivot:** The model uses Maya to execute a specific emotional beat: the "thawing" of a teenager. She is a reliable tool for transitioning a narrative from tension/isolation to warmth/connection.
* **Name-Space Rigidity:** The name "Maya" is rarely given a surname (99% are just "Maya"), suggesting she is treated more as a functional archetype than a fully realized individual.
